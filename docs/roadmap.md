# hdClaude roadmap and phase tracker

Status: live. Last revised 2026-09-08.

This is the durable progress record. Update the tracker, the evidence column,
and the decision log **in the same commit** as the work they describe. A phase
is not complete because its API exists; it is complete when its exit gate passes
and the evidence is recorded here.

## How to resume

1. Read the decision log and the phase tracker below.
2. Inspect the worktree and recent commits. **Never assume this document is
   newer than the code.**
3. Run `compile.bat` and the full test suite before changing renderer
   behaviour, and `render_gallery.bat` after.
4. Work one reviewable phase boundary at a time.
5. Re-render and commit the full gallery after every render-affecting change.
6. Record timing, image, validation, and hardware evidence in the phase entry.

Eight of the ten gallery scenes re-render byte-identical, since none of them enables the control. The analytic lights are emitters a scattered ray can reach: they stay out of the acceleration structure and are intersected in closed form, which is what a rect and a sphere already are, so a light occludes what is behind it and appears in a reflection. Next-event estimation and BSDF sampling therefore both reach them and are weighed by the balance heuristic. The partition is checked against a closed form rather than against a previous render: a 0.8 Lambertian under a rect emitter of radiance 3 at unit half-extents and height 2 renders 0.5716, where the point-to-rectangle configuration factor gives 0.5747 -- a total that double counting overshoots and an unmatched weight undershoots. A light seen directly reads the radiance it was given, 0.4967 against 0.50. A smooth dielectric reflects exactly its Fresnel share: under a uniform sky, viewed head on, it renders 0.0402 at IOR 1.5 against the closed form's 0.0400, 0.0455 at 1.54107 against 0.0453, and 0.1116 at 2.0 against 0.1111. And the MIS split conserves what it divides: under a rect light wide enough to swallow the whole specular lobe, a delta mirror and a 0.02-roughness gloss of the same IOR both render 0.0800 against the closed form's 0.0800, and so do both transmissive cases -- 0.0808 for the delta and 0.0810 for the gloss, which is the shader ball's glass exactly and the one combination where the two densities could have disagreed about whether a lobe's probability is folded into it -- a closure that dropped its reflection lobe once transmission was enabled would look exactly like a glass ball with a bare shell and would pass every reflection-only test -- the assertion that a near-mirror lobe needs, because the balance heuristic gives next-event estimation almost nothing there and a missing scattered-ray half would delete the highlight rather than roughen it, with every furnace still passing. That is the number a glass material is right or wrong by, and it is small enough that a plausible image survives getting it wrong -- glass reflecting a light at a twentieth of gold's strength looks much like glass not reflecting it at all. A scene of lights and no geometry now builds a valid empty top-level structure rather than a null descriptor. A transmissive material's interior is transported: `anisotropic_vdf` publishes an absorption coefficient and the traversal kernel applies Beer-Lambert over the flight it just measured, which is the only kernel that knows the distance. The transmittance is upsampled rather than the coefficient -- `exp(-sigma d)` is bounded in (0, 1] whatever the coefficient is, which is the range the reflectance fit is built for, and it keeps RGB becoming spectral at the closure boundary and nowhere else. Asserted as a *ratio* against the same surface with a clear interior, so whatever constant `open_pbr_surface` puts on transmission cancels and only the medium is under test: 0.6402, 0.3596, 0.1599 against the closed form's 0.6400, 0.3600, 0.1600. A medium also scatters: `extend` walks the path through it, sampling a free flight and a Henyey-Greenstein direction until it reaches a boundary, which turns honey from a clear amber into the cloudy material it is (mean 0.402 to 0.635). The walk lives in the traversal kernel because a scattering event has no material and so no bin in the per-material sort; a path still leaves that kernel with exactly one surface hit. Remaining on the volume: scattering is *achromatic*, because a chromatic coefficient sampled against one control wavelength makes every other lane carry a weight that grows with the flight and compounds over a walk until it overflows -- it produced NaN before being cut back -- and fixing that properly is multiple importance sampling across the lanes' densities. Colour in a medium therefore comes from absorption, which is spectral and exact. There is also no next-event estimation at a scattering vertex. The volumetric white furnace that would gate the walk -- a closed slab that absorbs nothing, in a uniform environment of unit radiance, must render exactly one however the light gets around inside it -- now gates a bare `dielectric_bsdf` slab, which renders 1.0001, 0.9987, 0.9979 -- the closure conserves energy exactly. A **sphere** furnace now stands beside it, and it is the first gate here that varies the angle at all: two flat quads present a delta refraction at one incidence per pixel and never a steep one, so every furnace before it was a near-normal measurement and an error that vanishes at normal incidence could not have been caught. The closures hold up -- a bare `dielectric_bsdf` reads 1.0065 and 0.9900 across the disc, `layer(R, T)` reads 1.0003 and 0.9894, and `layer(R, layer_vdf(T, anisotropic_vdf))` enclosing a vacuum reads what the layered dielectric reads, which puts any volume failure inside the volume rather than around it. The walk does too, now. A sphere of a lossless scattering medium read **1.4214** at an interface index of 1.5 and reads 1.0110; forward scattering, a dense medium and a chromatic one at four to one across the channels all land within 1.2 per cent of one and neutral, and the gate is asserted. The defect was not in the walk and not in the layering: `mx_dielectric_bsdf` handed the *absolute* index to both sides of the interface, so a path inside glass saw the reflectance curve of a path outside it -- a factor of two wrong at 35 degrees of internal incidence and a factor of twenty past the critical angle, where the true value is one. Nothing reached those angles before a scattering interior existed, since refraction maps the whole outside hemisphere into a cone of exactly the critical half-angle. Three things moved together: the Fresnel now gets the relative index, which is all `mx_fresnel_dielectric` ever needed to return its own total internal reflection; the directional albedo `layer` reads as an energy split is weighted by the Fresnel actually in force rather than by a Schlick fit that cannot climb to one before grazing; and the reflect/refract split's `[0.05, 0.95]` clamp is gone, since with a correct Fresnel the two conditions it guarded against coincide, and its ceiling was costing five per cent on every crossing past the critical angle. Asking *which side* was the hard part, and two wrong answers were caught by the gallery before the third was right: the shading normal tilts past the horizon near a silhouette, which put a rim of total internal reflection around every rounded opaque object in the subdivision scene, and `scatter_mode` cannot separate the reflection half of a transmissive interface from a coat over an opaque substrate. Only the path's own history answers it, so the shade kernel publishes both the geometric normal and whether the path is inside a medium, and a closure reads the inside curve only when a transmission event put it there. The closure validation suite gained internal incidences to pin it: 0.0859 against a closed form's 0.0860 at 35 degrees, 0.9980 against 1.0 past the critical angle. Two furnaces that already passed improved with it, the layered dielectric slab from 0.9953 to 0.9978. Spectral MIS across the lanes now ships, per *walk* rather than per step. Choosing the proposing lane once per step is unbiased and bounds each step's weight by the lane count, but a walk multiplies steps and the path's weight is then bounded only by the lane count raised to the step count: that form read 1.02, 1.06, 1.01 on a four-to-one medium and did not tighten at eight times the samples, while being exact on a medium thin enough to scatter once. Choosing once per walk applies the balance heuristic to the whole walk's density, which has a closed form -- `sigma_s[j]^collisions * exp(-sigma_s[j] * distance)`, since a product of exponentials is an exponential of a sum -- so the walk carries a collision count and a total distance and no per-step product at all, evaluated in logarithms with the largest term factored out. Roulette moved onto the accumulated absorption, the part of the weight that actually decays, with its compensation carried as a scalar to the end. It converges: the chromatic spread falls from 3.2 per cent at 256 samples to 1.4 at 2048, about the square root of the sample ratio, where the per-step form stayed near five at both. The gate reads 1.0075 isotropic, 1.0035 forward, 1.0067 dense, and 0.9991, 1.0037, 1.0085 chromatic at four to one. Three exact guards keep it attributable -- a path in vacuum draws nothing, scattering is tested on the *authored* coefficient rather than the upsampled one, which is a millionth rather than zero for a clear glass, and an achromatic medium draws nothing since its four techniques are one technique. Seven gallery scenes stay byte identical, Sponza moves by an RMS of 0.0037 and the glass ball and playground by 0.031 and 0.057, and every per-channel mean is unchanged to four decimal places: the movement is resampling, and no asset here has a medium chromatic enough to show the transport as a colour. The same slab built from `open_pbr_surface` read **1.1505** -- fifteen per cent gained per crossing -- and now reads 1.0145. The defect was in `mx_layer_bsdf`, which clamped the probability of selecting its top layer to a floor of 0.05: `open_pbr_surface` layers a coat and a fuzz over its base whether or not they are switched on, and a layer of zero weight has zero albedo, so three dead lobes were each claiming five per cent of a mixture density that no longer described the sampling. Zero is safe because the two conditions coincide -- a top layer with unit throughput has taken nothing, so its response is zero and there is nothing to weigh. Against hdCodex the glass ball falls from 0.145 to 0.113 and the playground from 0.248 to 0.236, which is the independent check. The residual 1.45 per cent was a four per cent loss and a five per cent gain that nearly cancelled, and both are fixed. The loss was `mx_layer_bsdf` applying `1 - F` twice, once as the reflection lobe's throughput and once inside the transmission lobe's own response; a transmission-only lobe exists to be layered under a reflection one, and MaterialX's convention is that a base does not know the top's Fresnel, so it no longer carries it. The gain was `mx_dielectric_bsdf` treating a single-lobe request as though it still had to choose, reflecting one sample in twenty nobody asked for and dividing its density by 0.95; the split now applies only in RT mode, where the closure genuinely has two lobes to weigh. Fixing either alone makes the image worse -- removing the gain alone takes the furnace to 0.9206 -- which is why they had to be found together. A lossless slab now renders 0.9953 against one, the layered surface agreeing with the bare closure's own noise floor, and the gate is tightened to two per cent. That is the same excess the medium's own test had to be written as a ratio to cancel. Energy conservation of a transmissive surface is therefore the next defect to fix, and the furnace goes in with it rather than being committed at a tolerance that would record the defect as correct. And subsurface is still not transported at all -- it is carried to the same medium machinery, but `mx_subsurface_bsdf` only ever evaluates the reflection closure and never samples a direction *into* the surface, so the hand-off is never reached and the bubblegum ball renders byte-identically to before. A conservation gate for it now exists ahead of the transport: a closed slab of `subsurface_bsdf` at unit albedo, whose radius carries the zero component the bubblegum asset authors, renders 0.9995 in a unit furnace. It passes today because subsurface behaves as a Lambertian, and it is there so that it still has to once a walk is carrying the light. **Subsurface is transported.** All three of the pieces this entry used to name as remaining are done, and a fourth defect turned up between them. The colour mapping is OpenPBR's own: `subsurface_bsdf`'s `color` is documented as the observed reflection colour, which is not the fraction of a collision that survives, and van de Hulst's inversion between them -- which OpenPBR states in closed form -- says a medium whose collisions each survive with probability 0.6 returns about 0.19 of what enters it. Handing the authored colour to a walk as its scattering albedo therefore renders every subsurface material about three times too dark, and looks like a lighting problem. The inversion is checked against the forward relation it inverts rather than against remembered values, and round trips to 4.7e-5 across the range at four anisotropies. The extinction is `1 / radius` with the regularization OpenPBR's text asks for in the limit `r -> 0`, which the bubblegum asset's `(1, 0, 0.068)` requires: a floor on the *ratio* between channels rather than an epsilon on the value, at the 18.42 that is the largest ratio the spectral mapping of a coefficient can carry, and invisible because a medium that dense is in the diffusive regime where van de Hulst's reflectance does not depend on the mean free path at all. A radius of zero in every channel is solved rather than regularized: the light leaves where it entered, so the closure is exactly a Lambertian of that reflectance. The publication question had a worse answer than expected. Both `standard_surface` and `open_pbr_surface` instantiate `subsurface_bsdf` unconditionally with `weight` hardwired to one and gate it downstream with a `mix`, and `standard_surface` has no `anisotropic_vdf` anywhere in its graph, so the old `subsurface_present && !medium_present` guard was true for every one of them: **every transmissive `standard_surface` in the gallery was filled with the default subsurface medium**, a dense lossless scattering interior nobody authored, and the Collective Project's glass eye was rendering as frosted glass. The medium now travels on the `BSDF` struct and is propagated by the same selection that propagates `sampledL`, because the question is not what a material published but what interior the lobe that carried the path encloses, and only the combinator that chose that lobe knows; `layer_vdf` is the one place it travels the other way, since that node is how MaterialX says a surface encloses an interior. The entry is a cosine lobe into the surface, and that is a recorded decision rather than an oversight: an index-matched interface does not deviate a ray, but a straight-through entry is a delta with no solid-angle density to report, and every surface model puts this node inside a `mix` whose combinator must report a mixture density. Both were implemented and measured on a sphere of a 0.6 material in a unit furnace -- undeviated 0.5577, cosine 0.6173, at 0.2 they read 0.1684 and 0.2093 -- and both conserve energy exactly, so the choice is about where the light goes and not how much of it there is. The fourth defect was found by the colour gate: van de Hulst's relation is steeply nonlinear where subsurface materials live, and applying it to three numbers *before* they became a spectrum made a magenta material render 0.5178, 0.1971, 0.4158 for an authored (0.6, 0.2, 0.4) -- a seventh short in red with the channels either side moving the other way. The reflectance is now published unconverted and inverted per wavelength, and the same material reads 0.6139, 0.2052, 0.4072, matching the achromatic probes to within the noise. Carrying that meant the medium travels as an extinction and a bounded colour rather than as two coefficients, since two coefficients resolved to wavelengths separately do not keep the ratio between them and that ratio is the whole colour of the material; what the colour means travels with it, because `anisotropic_vdf` describes an interior by a coefficient pair and `subsurface_bsdf` by the light that comes back out. Two adjacent errors had to be fixed to get there. The path state conflated "which side of the interface am I on" with "is there an interior to transport through" in one hardcoded bit, and a clear dielectric answers those differently -- it encloses nothing and still has an inside, which is the side a closure reads its relative index from -- so they are now separate; conflating them the other way took `layer(R, T)` from 0.9978 to 0.9955 by undoing the relative-index fix. And `hdclaude_lane_extinction` upsampled a coefficient's transmittance over one *scene unit*, so a coefficient of 30 arrived as 1e-13 and the clamp returned 18.4: every medium denser than about 18 per unit mapped to the same one, and a subsurface material is nothing but such media. It is now referred to the medium's own mean free path, which is the best-conditioned band the fit has and is the same answer at any scene scale. The gates: a closed sphere of unit-albedo subsurface reads 1.0018, 0.9984, 0.9983, the slab carrying the asset's own zero-radius component reads 1.0109, 1.0157, 1.0139, and the colour gate reads 0.6173 for 0.6 and 0.2093 for 0.2, with a three-hundredths tolerance that separates the shipped boundary from the identity mistake at 0.19 and from the undeviated entry at 0.5577. Five gallery scenes moved and each is explicable: Collective Project 001 by 0.0328 as its glass eye stops being frosted, the bubblegum ball by 0.0727 over 57 per cent of its pixels as subsurface transports for the first time, the chess set by 0.0148 as its stone bleeds like marble, the Playground by 0.0191 as its green jar and purple toy transport, and honey by 0.0351 as its albedo of (1.0, 0.552, 0.229) survives being resolved to wavelengths. Sponza, the gold and glass balls, the Kitchen Set, the subdivision matrix and the height map are byte identical, which is the attribution: nothing without an interior moved. Written up in [implementation-notes.md](implementation-notes.md). Absorption is also not carried on shadow rays, so a light sampled from inside glass arrives untinted, and a path that hits nothing while inside a medium is not attenuated -- the physical answer over an unbounded distance is zero, but reaching it means the medium was never closed. Next-event estimation samples the transmission closure as well as the reflection one, so a light seen *through* a refracting surface is estimated rather than left to a scattered ray that happens to point at it: a smooth dielectric with a rect light behind it renders 1.9242, and a 0.02-roughness one 1.9240, against the closed form's 1.9200 for `(1 - R(0)) * L`. Measured honestly, it did not reduce the noise on the Standard Shader Ball's glass at all -- 0.1072 against its own converged image at 64 samples both before and after -- because that glass is near-specular at 0.01625 roughness, so the balance heuristic correctly gives next-event estimation almost nothing on *either* branch. It pays where transmission is rough, and the gap it closed was real; it is not what makes this asset quiet. Dispersion is transported. The wavelength-to-index relation is a two-term Cauchy whose coefficients are fully determined by the nominal index and the Abbe number, reproducing BK7, SF11 and diamond at the d-line and their F-to-C spread to six decimals, and asserting the direction a smaller Abbe number spreads in, which is the sign error no image betrays. It cannot travel through the closure -- MaterialX 1.39.3's `open_pbr_surface` declares `transmission_dispersion_scale` and `transmission_dispersion_abbe_number`, threads both into the generated function's signature and never reads either, and `ND_dielectric_bsdf` has no dispersion input to receive them -- so it is read off the document instead, resolved to one effective Abbe number `V / scale` since OpenPBR's scale multiplies the dispersive power, and pushed to the shading dispatch, which is already per material and so carries a per-material value for nothing. A path meeting a dispersive interface keeps its hero lane at that wavelength's index and terminates the other three, because a path is one direction; the survivor is scaled by the lane count, since the film divides the packet by it, which makes the estimator the unbiased single-wavelength one and four times noisier. The compensation must happen exactly once and nothing else on a path records that it has -- three zero lanes are what a terminated path looks like too -- so the path carries a flag; without it a ray entering a slab and leaving it is four times too bright. Both failures are caught by a furnace rather than by an eye: a closed dispersive slab that absorbs nothing must still render one and still render neutral, and reads 0.9919, 0.9969, 1.0061. The magnitude is asserted against a closed form, because the visible effect is a faint fringe: a smooth RT dielectric mirrored back at a rect light reflects `R(lambda)` and transmits the rest into blackness, so at n = 1.5 and Abbe 20 the pixel reads 0.0759, 0.0811, 0.0876 against the integral's 0.0782, 0.0816, 0.0874, with blue over red at 1.154 against 1.118 -- the ratio asserted separately, since an inverted sign still tints a highlight and still matches a closed form computed with the same inverted sign. The limit is that only a lobe that transmits is dispersed, which is what the input's own name says and all that can be said safely, because a reflection-only dielectric is indistinguishable at runtime from a coat -- a different interface with no Abbe number of its own. MaterialX builds OpenPBR's specular lobe as `layer(reflection-only, transmission-only)`, so an `open_pbr_surface` glass refracts a full spectrum and reflects a highlight only partly tinted, 1.037 blue over red against the interface's 1.154; energy is unaffected, since the layer hands its base `1 - F` whatever the base's index. Two gallery scenes moved slightly and neither shades differently: `hdclaude_dispersed_ior(ior, 0, lambda)` returns its argument by an early return with no arithmetic at all, but `ior` is now assigned rather than folded, and a different schedule of the same arithmetic reroutes a handful of paths -- OpenChessSet by an RMS of 0.00049 and Collective Project 001 by 6.1e-05, both means unchanged to six digits. Superseded prerequisites: the closure ABI's `hdclaude_wavelengths` was wired to a fixed quartet rather than to the path's own hero wavelengths and is fixed, and `BSDF::spectrum` is still written by nothing and read by nothing, which dispersion turned out not to need. The renderer does **not** produce non-finite samples; the earlier claim that it did was a mis-invocation, and the renders containing them were made without `--colorCorrectionMode disabled`, so usdrecord applied an sRGB transfer function to a scene-linear AOV and a negative sample raised to a fractional power became a NaN. The flag now lives in `render_claude.bat` beside `--disableCameraLight`, since the sRGB transform belongs to the EXR-to-JPEG conversion and nowhere else, and every gallery image re-renders byte-identical with it moved. What stands from that investigation is the *gate's* blindness: it compares display-transformed JPEGs, so nothing the display transform normalises -- a non-finite value, a negative, any magnitude above one -- can reach it, and the EXR is the actual rendered data. `hdClaudeImageDiff` gained a `--scan` mode that reads one linear image and reports its true range, mean, non-finite count and negative count. It says two things at once. ## Exit gates

Each gate is a claim that can fail. "The code exists" is never a gate.

**Phase 0.** `compile.bat` configures and builds the core-only preset from a
clean clone with no manual dependency steps. `setup_usd_env.bat` reports a clear,
actionable failure for each missing dependency rather than failing later inside a
tool. All committed documents cross-reference consistently.

**Phase 1.** Unit tests pass with no GPU and no OpenUSD present. Spectral round
trip: sRGB primaries and a set of Munsell reflectances upsample and re-integrate
to within 1e-3 dE2000. The shader cache is proven to miss on every component of
its key.

**Phase 2.** A validation-enabled run of the full suite reports zero validation
errors **and the test fails if the count is nonzero** (rule R8). Validation here
means core *and* synchronisation validation: core validation checks that each
command is legal on its own and says nothing about whether what one kernel wrote
is visible to the next, which in a wavefront integrator is most of what can be
wrong. Device-loss
simulation leaves the latch set, every subsequent entry point refuses, and the
destructor completes without issuing a wait.

**Phase 3.** Prototype BLAS reuse, in-place TLAS update, and in-place BLAS update
under stable-topology deformation are each asserted by reading back GPU state,
not a CPU shadow (rule R7). Subdivision preserves creases, corners, holes,
orientation, face-varying seams, and material subsets against a committed
baseline -- and the baseline is not enough on its own. From 2026-09-06 to
2026-09-07 this gate passed against a baseline in which the creased cube was
fully rounded, because a baseline can only prove that nothing changed. The
gate is met when the claim is also asserted by something that does not
compare the renderer against itself.

**Phase 4.** All 22 pbrlib overrides present (the set is all-or-nothing; see
[materialx-codegen.md](materialx-codegen.md) §3), and the five acceptance items
in §8 of that document: every gallery material compiles with no fallback, white
furnace within 0.5%, chi-squared agreement between sampling and the reported
density, a finite nonzero density at every sampled direction, and combinator
mixture densities that integrate to one — for every closure **and every
combinator**. The last item is the one that catches a combinator reporting the
selected child's density instead of the mixture.

**Phase 5.** Per-material dispatch is observed to scale: adding a large
procedural nodegraph to one object changes that object's shading cost and not
the frame's. Queue counters are GPU-written and never read back by the CPU
during a frame.

**Phase 6.** Furnace test at scene scale. Analytic light comparisons for each
`UsdLux` type. Spectral: a dispersion scene matches an analytic reference; a
metamer pair renders as distinct under two illuminants.

**Phase 7.** A render-pass-level test drives move -> move -> stop -> move against
a stub scene and asserts a frame reaches the AOV on every moving frame (rule R7;
this is the test whose absence hid hdCodex A2/A1/N1/N8). Failure paths assert
`IsConverged()` stays false and the revision is retried.

**Phase 8.** Every gallery scene renders, and the image gate in the gallery
script passes against a committed baseline (rule R9). Timings, GPU, driver, and
TDR values recorded (rule R11). Every remaining difference from hdCodex is
*assessed against the specifications* and its resolution recorded -- which
renderer the specification agrees with, and why -- rather than closed by making
the images match. A difference that hdClaude is right about is a passing result.

**Phase 9.** GPU timestamps prove frame N+1's trace overlaps frame N's readback —
measured across a *queue*, not within one command buffer, since a whole-buffer
barrier's first synchronisation scope includes everything already submitted to
the queue. This is the exact claim hdCodex could not make (finding N3); it is
achievable here only because of Rule 1.

**Phase 10.** Jitter sequence, motion vectors, and history reset are validated
against a synthetic scene with known motion. Motion vectors are correct under
instancing and deformation, or the phase does not close.

**Phase 11.** The native fallback improves a fixed-sample interactive image
measurably (SSIM against the converged reference) on a device with no DLSS.

**Phase 12.** Runtime support query returns a correct answer on a supported and
an unsupported device, and the renderer runs unchanged when DLSS is absent.

**Phase 13.** DLAA at native resolution is compared against the converged
reference by SSIM and by a temporal-stability metric. Reference mode output is
bit-identical with DLSS present and absent.

**Phase 14.** Ray Reconstruction fed only by guides produced by MaterialX
closures (never by a surface-model name). Reference mode remains unaffected.

| 2026-09-07 | Invalid or mismatched scene input is reported, never coerced | A renderer that silently patches malformed input hides the defect from the person who can fix it and makes its own image untrustworthy, because you can no longer tell which pixels came from the asset and which from the workaround. | 2026-09-08 | An interior medium travels on the `BSDF` struct, selected with the direction | Both `standard_surface` and `open_pbr_surface` instantiate `subsurface_bsdf` and `anisotropic_vdf` whether or not the material uses either, so a global records whichever closure ran last. The path enters the interior belonging to the lobe that carried it through the interface, and only the combinator that chose that lobe knows which that was. | 2026-09-08 | A subsurface colour is inverted per wavelength, never per channel | van de Hulst's relation, which OpenPBR quotes to define `subsurface_color`, is steeply nonlinear, and a nonlinearity applied to an RGB triple before it becomes a spectrum does not commute with the projection back. Inverting the three numbers first left a magenta material a seventh short in red; upsampling the reflectance and inverting each lane makes the walk return the authored colour |
| 2026-09-08 | A medium is carried as an extinction and a bounded colour, not as two coefficients | Absorption and scattering resolved to wavelengths through separate fits do not keep the ratio between them, and that ratio -- the single-scattering albedo -- is the whole colour of a subsurface material. | 2026-09-08 | The subsurface entry is a cosine lobe, not the undeviated direction | An index-matched interface does not deviate a ray, but a straight-through entry is a delta with no solid-angle density, and every surface model puts this node inside a `mix` whose combinator must report a mixture density; the alternative is a narrow lobe of some invented width. | 2026-09-08 | A coefficient's transmittance is upsampled over the medium's own mean free path | At a fixed unit distance a coefficient of 30 arrives as a transmittance of 1e-13 and the clamp returns 18.4, so every medium denser than about 18 per unit mapped to the same one -- which is every subsurface material. ## Open questions

Tracked here rather than decided prematurely.

1. **Sort granularity.** Counting sort per bounce over all active paths, or a
   partial sort that only separates the few most expensive materials? Decide
   with a measurement in phase 5, not before.
2. **Shadow-ray batching depth.** One shadow queue flushed per bounce, or a
   deeper queue flushed per frame? Interacts with memory footprint at 4K.
3. **Displacement residency.** GPU displacement (phase 16) multiplies vertex
   memory by the refinement level. Whether to cache displaced positions or
   re-evaluate per BLAS build is a memory/time trade to measure.
4. **Volume rendering.** MaterialX VDFs are declared and generated; hdClaude
   currently plans homogeneous interior media only. Heterogeneous volumes
   (`UsdVol`) are not scheduled.
